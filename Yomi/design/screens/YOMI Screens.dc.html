N.03   NOVEL READER
VOL. 03 / CH. 27
The Weight of Small Numbers

The lantern guttered once and went still, and in that pause he understood the corridor was listening. Every archive keeps its own silence; this one had teeth.

He set his palm flat against the cold shelf and counted the spines by touch — forty, then forty-one — until the numbers stopped meaning anything and became a kind of prayer he had not intended to make.

Somewhere below, water moved through stone. He had been told the lower stacks flooded every spring, that whole decades of record dissolved into the dark and were simply, quietly, let go. Nobody grieved them. That, he decided, was the part he could not forgive.

So he began to read faster, as if speed were a form of rescue, as if a page held in memory were a page saved from the water.
